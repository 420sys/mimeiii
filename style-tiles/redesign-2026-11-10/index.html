Four desks,
one machine.

Four ways MIMEI could look, built from your whole board and your sixteen answers. Each one is the same machine you have now (four workspaces, the bar, the lesson, the manual, the terminal, the files, settings, search) with a different desk in front of it and a different hand on every page.

Nothing in the real site changed. Every number is from the course, the lesson is 3.1 word for word, and the progress shown belongs to a demo learner. Everything on screen is drawn in code.

01 · The board, measured

Nine tenths of your colour is teal or blue.

The seven you attached (moodboard/v6-lunar, 01 to 07)

Each image was shrunk and every pixel sorted. The thick bar is how the coloured pixels divide between hue families; the thin bar is tone (near-black, grey, colour, near-white); the strip is the eight most common colours. The numbers are in moodboard/v11-whole/palette.json, made by palette.py beside it.

The lavender and pink live in the vibe folder. In what you attached, and in what you answered, they are nearly absent, so they are absent here.

02 · What it says

Cold light, one enormous thing, one very fine thing.

Cold light in the dark

Colour is rarely the subject. Light is: windows, a billboard, a hologram, white-hot glass. Hue stays between teal and blue; brightness does the work.

Huge beside hair-thin

A hologram as tall as a tower over thousands of tiny windows. A crest over filigree. Almost nothing at a middle scale.

Lettering is architecture

Names on towers, billboards, vertical signs, script under a crest. Words are never a caption under the picture.

Ornament has two shapes

It grows out of one corner, or it stands symmetric like a coat of arms.

Two grounds

A fifth of what you attached is black and a fifth is white. Night and Ice are both native to the board.

Screens inside screens

The vibe folder is riced desktops, windows and terminals. The small Linux comes from there, and it stays.

Height, weather, nobody about

Rain, haze, snow. Seen from high up or from the street looking up. People absent or tiny.

A 2000s hand

Glossy black, shards, thin interface bars, micro type: the digital art of 2003 to 2008, drawn with care.

05 · How to look

Open one, then press things.

In the lesson, tick the last box of the runbook ("Ask some questions"): that reaches a checkpoint, and each direction answers in its own way. In the terminal on workspace 3, try help, ls, scene ice and open 3.1.

The small pill at the bottom of every prototype switches direction, workspace and scene. It belongs to no design.

Each was checked at 1440 × 900 (MacBook) and 2560 × 1440 (big monitor).

06 · What I need from you next

Pick one, or name the parts.

  1. Which desk? A, B, C, D, or stay with today's. Mixing is fine: "A's desk with B's pages" is a valid answer.
  2. The warm lamp. A and C keep today's one warm light that marks you. B and D have none: teal, ice, white and black only. Which is right?
  3. Which Ice? Each direction reads Ice differently: a snowed-in dawn (A), white paper and black ink (B), signs switched off in the snow (C), daylight through the glass (D).
  4. How loud is too loud? Is any desk more than you want to open every night? Is any lesson page not calm enough to read for two hours?
  5. The beads. D draws all 210 checkpoints round the window and lights the ones reached. Is that still "checkpoints only", or does it cross into a picture that fills in?
  6. Type. Any face you want kept, or never want to see again?
